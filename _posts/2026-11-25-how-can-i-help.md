If you're a new grad about to enter software right now, I can see how confusing it is.

Everything around you is loud. One post says AI will make junior engineers obsolete. The next says CS degrees are worthless. It's hard to tell what's real, what's foundational, and what's written for clicks. A lot of that noise comes from people like me, already in the industry, who should know better. We stopped being helpful to the people coming in after us. I’m sorry for my part in that, and I’d like to help fix it where I can.

So here's what I'm going to do. I'm going to make time to talk with new grads one-on-one and answer the questions you actually have, without the hype or the doom. When I don't know something, I'll say so and try to find someone who does. I've been building software for about 20 years and an Amazon Bar Raiser. Views shared will be my own. I can't speak for every company, but I can share principles that hold up, and I know Amazon's process best. Here's what I can help with:

* What to focus on when preparing for interviews (I can't speak for every company)
* What to expect in interviews (I can't speak for every company)
* How to think about technical design
* How to use AI tools while you're learning without skipping the fundamentals
* What to learn while you're still in school
* What makes an engineer successful over the long run
